Switchyard · design proposal

Connectors, rebuilt around the service

Today the page is a list of engine accounts, each with a table of MCP server names inside. You have to know that “Linear for Codex” and “Linear for Claude” are separate rows in separate boxes, and a session that needs one just fails mid-turn. This proposal makes the service the unit: one card per connector, and the same card can appear in a conversation when an agent needs it.

1One card per service

Linear is one card, not a row per account and source. The card says where it works; the account detail is one click in.

2Sorted by what needs you

Needs you, then Connected, then Available. No filter drawer, no pagination: search covers the long tail.

3Connect from the conversation

An agent that needs a connector puts its card in the thread. You click, sign in, and the turn carries on.

1 · The page

Cards are grouped by state. Each shows the accounts it works on as engine marks: solid means connected, an amber dot means that account needs a sign-in, dashed means not added there.

6 · What this asks of the build

The design leans on what yardd already does. These are the parts that are new, and the calls I made that you may want to overrule.

Merging rows into one service

“claude.ai Linear”, “plugin:linear:linear” and a config entry named “linear” become one Linear card, matched by address first and by name second. Anything unmatched keeps its own card with a letter tile.

New: a small catalog of known services (name, mark, one-line description) in the UI. No daemon change.

The agent’s request

A request_connector tool on Switchyard’s own MCP server, and a new thread item for the card. Claude sessions get it first; Codex gets the automatic card on a failed call.

New: one tool, one event, one item kind. The sign-in itself is today’s mcp.auth.

An agent can ask, only you can allow

An agent’s connector list stays owner-only. Its request shows “Let Firefighter use Sentry?” with Allow, Just this conversation and Deny. Nothing is granted by the request alone.

Open question: whether “Just this conversation” is worth the extra state.

Unattended runs do not block

An automation with nobody watching does not wait on a card. It goes on without the connector, says so in its run, and the Connectors nav item shows a count.

Open question: whether some automations should wait and notify instead.
